## Broad Topic Calibration

Use this corpus as style evidence, not as a topic cage.

- Management Science is broad enough for strategy, entrepreneurship, innovation, IT, organizations, accounting, finance, marketing, operations, and individual or organizational decision making. A good paragraph can therefore begin from almost any management decision if it reaches rigor and decision relevance.
- Operations Research explicitly spans methods such as decision analysis, optimization, simulation, stochastic modeling, machine learning, data science, markets, revenue management, and empirical rigor, plus application domains such as energy, finance, supply chains, societal impact, and transportation.
- M&SOM and related OM papers often make the abstract readable by separating problem definition, method and results, and managerial implications. The learning recommendations paper is a useful model for learning and bandit topics because it explains the business friction before the bandit formulation.
- Public writing skills and university writing guides converge on the same practical edits: active voice when it clarifies agency, concrete nouns, one job per paragraph, no needless words, and removal of generic AI filler.

## Recurring But Conditional Relations

Many papers in this corpus are practice-led analytical papers, so several relations recur. They are observations about that subset, not a universal story pattern.

- A practice decision can provide an accessible opening when the application is needed to interpret the model.
- A standard intuition or model matters only when the paper actually qualifies, extends, or contradicts it.
- A tractable formal object can follow an operational constraint, or it can open a technical paper and receive its application immediately afterward.
- Conditional results should name the parameter, constraint, market condition, or policy class that controls the conclusion.
- An implication needs a decision maker and observable condition only when the paper supports actionable advice.
- A benchmark should be memorable when the contribution is comparative. It may be the status quo, no regulation, a traditional design, a myopic policy, or a standard algorithm.
- Social consequences should be represented at the evidence's actual strength. Use tradeoffs when outcomes conflict; do not invent one when the result is monotone.

## Paper Cards

### Price Discrimination With Fairness Constraints

Story: personalized pricing is common; fairness concerns arise when protected groups face different prices or access. The paper turns a broad fairness debate into precise constraints and then asks which constraints help or hurt welfare.

Style lessons:

- The introduction moves from practice to evidence to regulation to research questions.
- Definitions come early because "fairness" is ambiguous.
- Results are framed as warnings against simplistic policy: more fairness is not always better, and different fairness notions behave differently.
- The best paragraphs name both sides of the tradeoff: seller profit, consumer surplus, and social welfare.

### Loot Box Pricing And Design

Story: loot boxes are a familiar digital monetization strategy; the seller chooses both price and design, while consumers and regulators care about surplus and manipulation.

Style lessons:

- The abstract and introduction make the business object concrete before modeling it.
- The main comparison is easy to remember: traditional versus unique loot boxes.
- Technical asymptotics are translated into business stakes: revenue optimality, near-zero surplus, and regulation of probability disclosure.
- The prose uses sharp benchmarks to make math legible.

### Matchmaking Strategies For Player Engagement

Story: engagement depends on repeated match outcomes, not just one-shot match quality. The platform controls matches, pay-to-win options, and bots.

Style lessons:

- The narrative puts the platform's dynamic control problem before the model.
- The key contrast is short-term match reward versus long-term skill distribution.
- Counterintuitive results are explained by an endogenous state variable, not by surprise language.
- Empirical or case-study evidence is positioned as validation of the mechanism and practical magnitude.

### Herding, Learning, And Incentives For Online Reviews

Story: review incentives affect both the number of reviews and what future consumers infer from them. The seller's best incentive policy depends on product quality and margin.

Style lessons:

- Define behavioral mechanisms plainly before the model: herding draws consumers to popularity; learning lets reviews signal quality.
- Compare policies in parallel so the reader remembers the decision menu.
- Managerial implications are conditional, not universal.
- Platform policy implications are separated from seller incentives.

### Pricing Reusable Resources Under Ambiguity

Story: classic dynamic pricing assumes known demand and often simple service times; cloud computing and parking require pricing shared reusable resources when demand and service time are uncertain.

Style lessons:

- The introduction earns abstraction by naming concrete applications.
- The gap is technical but explained in plain operational terms: requests stay in the system for uncertain time.
- Contributions combine model, approximation, and data-motivated numerical tests.
- The prose carefully distinguishes optimal dynamic policies, fixed-price heuristics, and out-of-sample performance.

### Learning Recommendations With Customer Disengagement

Story: bandit algorithms learn by experimenting, but bad recommendations can cause customers to leave, shortening the learning horizon.

Style lessons:

- Structured abstract format is extremely clear: problem, relevance, methodology, results, managerial implications.
- The core tension is told in everyday terms before the generalized linear bandit appears.
- Strong result language is paired with a mechanism: classical algorithms overexplore because they ignore disengagement.
- The recommendation is practical and conditional: constrain exploration when disengagement risk is high.

### Dynamic Stochastic Matching Under Limited Time

Story: matching platforms face a timing problem: waiting can make the market thicker, but participants may abandon before a better match arrives.

Style lessons:

- The opening turns a technical online matching problem into an operational choice about timing.
- The central tradeoff is simple and memorable: market thickness versus abandonment risk.
- Algorithmic results are framed against familiar operational benchmarks such as batching.
- Experiments with real taxi demand translate worst-case guarantees into practical cost efficiency.

### Improving Match Rates In Dating Markets

Story: an online dating platform must decide which potential partners to show to each user, and match formation depends on behavior from both sides over time.

Style lessons:

- The structured abstract is a strong model for empirical/algorithmic OM papers: problem definition, relevance, methodology, results, managerial implications.
- The paper earns its field experiment by first explaining the operational metric and partner context.
- Methodology is layered clearly: model, estimate preferences and behavior, design heuristics, validate by simulation and field experiments.
- The managerial takeaway is not "use algorithms"; it is to account for preferences, activity, and history on both sides of the market.

### Multitier Inventory With Expediting And Disruptions

Story: a partner's distribution network has central and regional inventory, expediting, stochastic demand, and supplier disruptions. The paper builds a parsimonious model that keeps the salient operational features and then validates policies on partner data.

Style lessons:

- The model section first maps the physical network into tiers, locations, decisions, lead times, and disruption states.
- The body keeps the stochastic-program lower bound, heuristic policy, and primary centralized-versus-decentralized insight.
- Partner-specific estimation, decensoring, lead-time fitting, extra disruption scenarios, and policy implementation details naturally move to appendices.
- Applied rigor comes from showing why the simplified model is a faithful approximation, not from putting every data-processing step in the body.

### Threatened-Species POMDP And Optimal Control

Story: conservation managers must survey and protect a species under uncertainty about occupancy, detectability, and dynamics. The paper turns an intractable Bayes-adaptive POMDP into a lower-dimensional reformulation.

Style lessons:

- Domain concepts are translated into state, belief, action, reward, and information before the dynamic program appears.
- The body keeps the reformulation because it is the main methodological object.
- The writing earns abstraction by explaining why a simpler occupancy state is appropriate for sparse detections.
- Case-study extensions and data files support implementation, but the body carries the state/belief/action logic.

### Optimal Genetic Testing Of Families

Story: testing one family member can reveal risk information for others, so the decision is a family-level dynamic testing plan rather than a one-person rule.

Style lessons:

- The abstract separates problem definition, structural result, numerical case study, and managerial implications.
- The threshold structure belongs in the body because it is the result readers must remember.
- Online appendix proofs are acceptable only after the body defines the policy and explains why family information changes the optimal sequence.
- Healthcare papers need extra translation from medical terminology into state, reward, and stopping decisions.

### Committing To Customer Orders In Online Retail

Story: online retailers must choose when to commit inventory to orders. The tradeoff is waiting time versus fill rate under customer order lead times.

Style lessons:

- The model section defines time, order arrivals, requested shipment times, replenishment, and backorders before stating policies.
- Definitions such as sensible policies stay in the body when later theorems rely on them.
- A proof can move to the appendix, but the body still explains why a stronger result is introduced and how it implies the displayed theorem.
- Result paragraphs transition from one performance metric to the next instead of listing theorems mechanically.

### Welfare-Improving Privacy Management

Story: privacy regulation changes firm strategy and consumer welfare through opt-in and data-use mechanisms. The paper analyzes a baseline model in the body and uses a general model to verify scope.

Style lessons:

- Baseline models belong in the body when they carry the mechanism cleanly.
- A general model can move to an appendix if the body tells the reader what the generalization preserves.
- Missing proofs can go to an online appendix, but result interpretation and welfare comparisons stay in the body.
- Policy implications are conditional and tied to welfare objects rather than broad privacy language.

### Coproductive Principal-Agent Dynamics

Story: a principal first decides whether to form a team and then how work is executed. The key inefficiency may arise from team formation rather than effort elicitation.

Style lessons:

- Benchmarks are layered: first-best, second-best, and contract forms appear before managerial claims.
- Short proof arguments can stay in the body when they clarify the comparative static.
- Envelope and implicit-function arguments should be named when they drive a result.
- Managerial implications work because they identify the distorted decision, not only the distorted effort level.

### Electricity Imbalance Pricing

Story: market rules for imbalance pricing affect market power and efficiency as renewable and low-cost generation grows.

Style lessons:

- The body defines the mechanisms being compared before equilibrium or efficiency regions.
- Theorems with regions need immediate prose and often a figure to make the parameter comparison legible.
- Observations after theorems are useful when they translate many cases into a managerial or regulatory pattern.
- Simulations complement theorem comparisons by putting all mechanisms on the same performance scale.

### EV Battery Swapping Location And Inventory

Story: deploying battery-swapping stations requires location, inventory, service, and repair decisions under urban demand.

Style lessons:

- Location-inventory papers need to state the physical service system before optimization machinery.
- Data files and online appendices are part of reproducibility, not a substitute for body-level model explanation.
- Algorithmic complexity should be tied to the planning decision and the scale of the city deployment problem.
- The first body sections should make the reader see the facility, battery, repair, and service-flow objects before equations become dense.

### Collaborative Learning And Decision Making

Story: multiple teams make sequential, mutually dependent pricing and recommendation decisions, but each team may not observe the other's decision process or model belief.

Style lessons:

- The body first makes the organizational decision process concrete before formulating a contextual bandit model.
- The proof idea is not stylized. It lists the regret-analysis steps, identifies the key eigenvalue growth argument, and then states the theorem.
- Theorem interpretation compares the rate with standard pricing and contextual bandit results, and states the assumptions under which the comparison is valid.
- Online appendix pointers are precise: they send sufficient conditions, lower bounds, omitted lemmas, and model-misspecification extensions to EC sections after the body explains their role.

### Payment For Ecosystem Services Contracts

Story: PES designers need simple contracts when landowners have private baseline conservation levels and optimal contracts are informationally demanding.

Style lessons:

- The proof sketch is very plain. It states a simplifying monotone-hazard-rate assumption, points to the appendix for the proof without that assumption, constructs an upper bound, and compares the proposed contract to that bound.
- The proof sketch uses equations only for the load-bearing comparison. It does not narrate every algebraic simplification.
- The numerical section interprets theorem regimes rather than merely validating the theorem.
- This is a good MS model for proof-idea voice: short, literal, and low-style.

### Long-Term Treatment Effects

Story: an online platform wants to estimate long-term treatment effects even though the experiment observes outcomes only for a shorter duration.

Style lessons:

- The model section builds levels of assumptions. Each stronger assumption is introduced because the previous identification strategy has a practical limitation.
- Theorem interpretation immediately states what the identification expression uses and why the linear surrogate model mitigates sample-size issues.
- Estimation follows the theorem by constructing plug-in estimators and explaining which quantities are observable, estimated, or replaced.
- Practical guidelines and algorithmic details are pointed to the Online Appendix after the body explains the estimator.

### Theory Of Privacy

Story: privacy changes consumer welfare through different market responses to information, and the sign depends on what information changes.

Style lessons:

- The body decomposes the effect into interpretable forces; the appendix verifies expansions and case splits.
- Appendix proofs are straightforward and literal: expand the expression, identify concavity or sign, split cases, and map the result back to the proposition.
- Extensions in the appendix still get interpretation. The extension explains which part of the main mechanism survives and what policy implication changes.
- This is a useful reminder that appendix writing can be dense without being cryptic.

### Relatively Robust Multicriteria Decisions

Story: decision makers need a compromise solution when criterion weights are uncertain or difficult to justify.

Style lessons:

- The main text develops axioms and propositions, while the appendix carries contradiction cases, compactness arguments, and recursive proof details.
- The proof voice is direct: define a set, show convexity or compactness, apply an existing theorem, and conclude the proposition.
- The conclusion translates the formal index into resource-allocation language, keeping the management object visible after technical development.
- This is a good pattern for formal papers where proof details are necessary but not central to the body narrative.

### Off-Grid Solar And Storage Investment

Story: off-grid renewable investment must jointly choose generation and storage under intermittent supply and nonbacklogged demand.

Style lessons:

- The introduction earns the model by explaining why storage is needed before mathematical investment decisions appear.
- The body distinguishes a general model from tractable newsvendor-like approximations that provide analytical insight.
- Calibration with geographically diverse data tests accuracy and turns approximate solutions into management insight.
- The proof/appendix split should preserve approximation logic in the body and move calibration detail or long derivations out.

### Reward Uncertainty In Social Referrals

Story: platforms use referral rewards to acquire and engage users, but uncertainty affects senders and recipients differently because referral decisions involve both private reward preferences and anticipated social reactions.

Style lessons:

- The introduction moves from common platform practice to a role-specific behavioral friction, then to a clean two-sided experimental design.
- Findings are sequenced as effect, behavioral asymmetry, mechanism, referral quality, and heterogeneity.
- Mechanism prose is concrete: guilt, fairness, social pressure, traffic source, sharing channel, and reward size each explain a bounded part of the result.
- The contribution paragraph is not just "large experiment"; it states what the experiment separates that prior uncertainty and referral literatures could not separate.

### Collaboration And Workplace Practice Adoption

Story: performance-enhancing workplace practices may fail not because managers lack formal authority, but because managers and workers need coordination and cooperation to implement the practice.

Style lessons:

- The opening starts from a broad management question and immediately defines the focal construct in operational terms.
- The design paragraph explains the partner organization, intervention, randomization, treatment content, and outcome metrics before results.
- Heterogeneity is written as a theory of when collaboration works: managerial information, initial safety position, span of control, turnover, and market competition.
- The paper teaches how to write field-experiment context: institutional details appear when they identify the treatment, outcome, or mechanism.

### Crowdsourcing Features In Mobile Apps

Story: crowdsourcing features can improve engagement and retention, but their components can interact negatively when content access reduces users' perceived control over co-creation.

Style lessons:

- Result narration separates two outcomes, engagement and retention, instead of treating "performance" as a single object.
- The interaction result is explained by a mechanism that links the product feature to user expectations.
- The managerial paragraph is conditional: add crowdsourcing features only with attention to how content submission and content access interact.
- The language does not rely on "surprise"; it names the expected complementarity and then explains why the interaction is negative.

### Incentive Misalignments In Programmatic Advertising

Story: ad platforms optimize under CPA contracts, but targeting consumers likely to purchase is not the same as targeting consumers whose purchase probability is increased by ads.

Style lessons:

- The paper builds a two-dimensional decision space: baseline purchase probability and incremental ad effectiveness.
- The empirical design is introduced after the model clarifies why the firm's and platform's objectives can diverge.
- Contributions move from contract incentives, to identification, to welfare, to a contract-design implication.
- This is a strong pattern for hybrid model-plus-experiment writing: theory states what must be measured; the experiment shows where consumers lie in the relevant space.

### Generative AI And High-Skilled Work

Story: firms are adopting coding assistants, but individual experiments are noisy; combining randomized trials across companies gives a sharper estimate of productivity effects and heterogeneity.

Style lessons:

- The abstract is unusually compressed: setting, randomized trials, sample, combined estimate, heterogeneity.
- It does not oversell "AI transformation"; it reports a measured productivity outcome and who benefits more.
- For rapidly evolving technology topics, MS style is calmer when the claim stays close to the experimental design and metric.

### Managerial Intervention And Collaboration

Story: delegation theory emphasizes motivation from nonintervention, but intervention can motivate when the principal's effort complements the agent's effort.

Style lessons:

- The abstract starts by naming the received theoretical view, then introduces the countervailing mechanism.
- The model is framed around when a managerial practice is or is not motivational, not around proving a model for its own sake.
- This is a good theory-paper pattern: standard belief, new friction, model, regime comparison, implication for management practice.

### Credit Rating Purchases And Index Membership

Story: widely used market indexes appear objective, but membership decisions contain discretion that may interact with firms' purchases of ratings from the index provider.

Style lessons:

- Empirical finance prose can still follow MS story logic: taken-for-granted institution, hidden discretion, empirical association, rule-change evidence, governance concern.
- The result language stays cautious with "appear to" and "raise concerns" when the mechanism is suggestive rather than directly observed.
- The contribution is strongest when the institutional implication is named: transparency and conflicts of interest in index construction.

### Designing Inclusive Offerings

Story: inclusivity is widely discussed in product and service design, but the assortment optimization literature lacks a formal definition and metric for inclusive offerings.

Style lessons:

- The abstract moves through definition, measures, optimization problem, hardness, algorithm, and application.
- Formal definitions are justified by a practical measurement gap before the optimization problem appears.
- The application to foundation shades makes the formal object concrete without turning the paper into a case study.
- This is a strong pattern for papers introducing a new construct: define it, measure it, optimize it, and show where it matters.

### Reproducibility In Management Science

Story: credibility in management research depends on whether published results can be reproduced, and reproducibility varies by data access, methodology, and disclosure regime.

Style lessons:

- The introduction defines reproducibility and replicability before reporting the study design.
- The paper's unit of analysis is the published article, so the data section must explain sampling, reviewer expertise, and obstacles to reproduction.
- Results are reported in a way that separates conditional reproducibility from all-article reproducibility.
- This is useful for writing meta-science or methods papers: define the standard, measure compliance, then explain heterogeneity by institutional constraint.

### Contractual Screening With Limited Memory

Story: standard sequential screening assumes a buyer can use prior private information later, but limited memory changes contract choice and creates a flat-rate bias.

Style lessons:

- The model section starts from a standard setup and then adds the new behavioral feature.
- Exposition explains who the seller and buyer represent before notation.
- Simplifying assumptions are paired with extensions that show which results survive.
- Comparative statics are easier to read when the prose first names the behavioral object being measured.

### Learning To Be Fair

Story: fair decision making should be evaluated through the consequences of actions under budget and parity preferences, not only through statistical constraints on predictions.

Style lessons:

- The paper makes fairness operational by tying it to a policymaker's objective and resource constraint.
- Stylized examples and Pareto frontiers are used to teach the tradeoff before the general policy-learning procedure.
- Simulation results compare learning approaches with oracle and random-assignment benchmarks.
- This is a strong pattern for normative algorithm papers: define stakeholder preferences, show why common fairness constraints can be inefficient, then learn policies under the explicit objective.

### Dynamic Inventory Management On Networks

Story: dynamic inventory control on networks becomes difficult because fulfillment flexibility breaks the classic mapping from backlog to resource and therefore weakens the usual base-stock logic.

Style lessons:

- The introduction uses a concrete network example to teach the state-space problem before the theorem appears.
- The contribution order is technical but still reader-friendly: lower bound family, implementable policy class, approximation guarantee, and numerical performance.
- The proof idea is part of the main story because the lower-bound and upper-bound stochastic programs explain why the guarantee is credible.
- This is a reminder that technical MS papers may need examples and formal objects early; forcing a long managerial motivation first would make the paper weaker.

### Sourcing With Demand Updates

Story: retailers place an early low-cost order under high demand uncertainty and can later place a costlier order after a signal updates mean demand.

Style lessons:

- The abstract moves from operational timing to forecast evolution model, analytical characterization, asymptotic heuristic, calibration, and regime-dependent benchmark comparison.
- The adjusted critical-fractile heuristic matters when the signal is more uncertain. When the signal is precise, the classic newsvendor solution can perform well.
- This is a good pattern for stochastic-model papers where the story is not "new method beats old method" but "which policy works under which information regime."

### Transfer, Cross-Learning, And Co-Learning With ODA

Story: limited operational data make transfer learning and data pooling tempting, but prediction-oriented transfer may not preserve decision optimality for the focal system.

Style lessons:

- The paper starts from decision making under limited data rather than from machine-learning novelty.
- It separates transfer learning, cross-learning, and co-learning by the relationship between related systems, focal systems, aggregate performance, and subsystem performance.
- The language ties domain knowledge to structural relationships between data and decisions.
- This is useful for data/ML-adjacent MS writing: avoid saying "use more data"; say how related data preserve or distort the decision structure.

### Redesigning VolunteerMatch's Search Algorithm

Story: a platform's efficiency-oriented ranking algorithm can repeatedly expose the same opportunities, leaving other opportunities without access to volunteers.

Style lessons:

- Equity is operationalized before the algorithm: weekly number of opportunities with at least one connection.
- The algorithm is both a theoretical object and a field intervention, so the paper must explain guarantee and implementation.
- The field result is scaled back to the platform's operational objective: more opportunities receive at least one connection without reducing total connections.
- This is a strong hybrid pattern for algorithm papers with partner implementation: old objective, distributional consequence, new objective, algorithm, experiment, scale.

### One-Way Mirrors In Online Dating

Story: information visibility in online dating changes matching behavior because users respond not only to preferences but also to weak signals generated by profile views.

Style lessons:

- The platform feature is concrete before the causal mechanism appears.
- The experiment identifies the behavioral mechanism behind higher matching outcomes, rather than merely reporting a platform metric.
- This is a useful older MS model for interface experiments: feature, information channel, user response, match outcome, mechanism.

### Front-Line Management Theory

Story: mid- and low-level managers do more than monitor; they adjust assignments under restricted incentive tools and limited wage authority.

Style lessons:

- The abstract is compressed because the model's object is clear: interaction between front-line manager and worker.
- The theoretical contribution is a characterization of managerial style as a function of patience and information, not a long list of managerial advice.
- This is a reminder that theory papers can be short and spare. They need enough context to interpret the formal departure and characterization, not a full empirical-style motivation sequence or a mandatory missing-channel paragraph.

## Cross-Corpus Language Rules

- Make the first-page object concrete enough to identify, whether it is an actor, institution, formal problem, construct, counterexample, or theorem object.
- Introduce a tension only when the paper has a real competing force, missing feature, or unresolved comparison; do not manufacture a second-beat friction.
- Put methods where their object and role are legible. Prior context may justify a concise method-first statement.
- Interpret theorem results through the relation they establish: characterization, comparison, condition, bound, impossibility, or mechanism. No fixed condition-mechanism-implication order is required.
- Use numbers only when they anchor a memorable benchmark; otherwise, keep the result qualitative until the formal statement.
- In empirical/field-experiment papers, say what was estimated, how the estimate changes the decision, and how the policy was validated.
- When the result overturns an intuition, make both the intuition and the state variable, constraint, or incentive that overturns it clear; either may come first.
- When several stakeholders matter, give each one a sentence-level role. Do not merge firm profit, consumer welfare, and regulator goals into a single vague implication.
- For full MS papers, keep a stable manuscript contract from abstract to discussion: central object, paper-level claim, comparator, metric, evidence owner, and boundary. Decision, friction, and mechanism belong only when the paper actually relies on them. Sections need stable relations, not a repeated story sentence.

## Red Flags

- Starting with "We consider a model..." when neither the sentence nor nearby context identifies the model's object or role.
- Calling a result "surprising" without explaining which intuition it overturns.
- Presenting managerial implications that do not follow from a theorem, estimate, or simulation.
- Letting related work become a citation list instead of a synthesis and relation map.
- Treating proof exposition as detached verification when first-pass trust requires the load-bearing reason the result is true.
